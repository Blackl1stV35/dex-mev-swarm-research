Confidence labels: **[S]** = confirmed this session via WebSearch; **[R]** = from my own training knowledge (cutoff Jan 2026), not re-verified this session — treat exact titles/arXiv IDs as needing a confirmation pass before formal citation; **[OPEN]** = no confirming or disconfirming literature found/recalled.

| # | Paper's question (paraphrased) | Done? | Later literature / how |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What forms does arbitrage/frontrunning take in **centralized** exchanges, and at what volumes? | Partially | CEX-side frontrunning/arbitrage measurement stayed thin in public academic literature through my knowledge cutoff; most follow-on work (below) instead measured the DEX/MEV side far more precisely than the CEX side, so this specific CEX-volume question is still largely open **[OPEN/R]**. |
| 2 | What techniques measure CEX trading activity without direct data access? | Not directly addressed | No dedicated technique paper recalled; this remains open. CEX-DEX **arbitrage** (as opposed to pure CEX activity) has since been measured indirectly via on-chain execution patterns correlated with CEX price moves — see row 4 **[R]**. |
| 3 | What financial incentives do CEXes create for DEX malfeasance, and what's the stability impact? | Substantially advanced | CEX-DEX arbitrage was later shown to be the **dominant** value source in MEV-Boost builder auctions (work on "who wins block-building auctions and why", builder/searcher ecosystem studies circa 2023-2024) — i.e. the paper's hypothesis that cross-venue price differences subsidize the ordering market was confirmed empirically at the builder-auction layer, not just the DEX-DEX layer the original paper measured **[R, exact citation needs a confirmation pass — recall points to Wahrstätter et al. and related Flashbots-research-team empirical work]**. |
| 4a | Can tight bounds be put on total MEV on Ethereum today? | Yes | "Quantifying Blockchain Extractable Value: How dark is the forest?" (Qin, Zhou, Gervais; IEEE S&P 2022) is the direct successor measurement paper — systematically quantifies arbitrage, sandwich attacks, and liquidations at scale, explicitly building on this paper's "pure revenue" framing and extending it to non-pure-revenue MEV **[R, high confidence on existence/framing, exact venue/year worth a one-line confirmation]**. Ongoing empirical tracking also exists via Flashbots' MEV-Explore-style dashboards **[R]**. |
| 4b | How much larger is the full arbitrage economy than what's executed (opportunities not pure revenue)? | Partially | "On the Just-In-Time Discovery of Profit-Generating Transactions in DeFi Protocols" (Qin, Zhou, Livshits, Gervais; IEEE S&P 2021) used automated/RL search to find profitable transaction sequences beyond the hand-identified "pure revenue" category this paper restricted itself to, directly extending the unexecuted-opportunity question **[R]**. |
| — | Sandwich attacks specifically (not pure revenue, but the paper's clearest non-pure-revenue MEV example) | Yes | Dedicated measurement work exists on sandwich-attack prevalence and victim losses on Ethereum DEXes (e.g. high-frequency-trading-on-AMMs-style studies, Zhou/Qin/Gervais-adjacent work) — quantifies what the original paper left as "other sources of value" **[R]**. |
| — | Do arbitrageurs collude with miners/builders? Is there incentive to avoid collusion? | Still mostly open empirically | The paper found no observed collusion in 2019 ("bot transactions are equally distributed across mining pools"). Post-Merge, the question has shifted shape: **builder/relay centralization** is a de facto coordination mechanism even without explicit collusion (a handful of builders/relays route most value), which is a structurally similar outcome to what the paper worried collusion would cause, confirmed by the relay-share data in `ethereum_architecture_security_survey.md` §2 **[S, via this session's search]**. Explicit miner-arbitrageur collusion (vs. builder-market concentration) has no confirmed empirical answer I can cite **[OPEN]**. |
| — | Are PGAs positive- or negative-sum games? Can the model be enriched? | Advanced, not closed | Later formal work extended PGA-style sealed/continuous-bid auction models with more realistic latency and N>2 player structure; this is exactly the gap our `swarm_pga_model.md` targets (N-player generalization, which the original paper explicitly flags as future work in its footnote on restricting to n=2) **[R, our own contribution planned here]**. |
| — | Can time-bandit attack opportunity size be estimated? | No | Original paper explicitly deferred this ("estimating the size of these opportunities is a challenging problem we defer to future work"). No dedicated empirical sizing paper recalled or found this session **[OPEN]**. This connects to problem 14 (LVR capture division) in our own repo, which addresses a related but narrower searcher/proposer value-split question, not the full time-bandit/history-rewriting threat. |
